Career / Identity / Design Leadership

Build the point of view. Then build the brand around it.

This is a preparatory guide for Amit Kumar Tiwari to clarify, test and reinforce his positioning as a design leader working at the intersection of product, systems, AI, people, community and education.

15+ yearsDesign leadershipAI × UXSystems thinkingCommunityEducation

01 / North star

The brand should make one thing increasingly obvious: what kind of leader Amit is, what he sees that others may miss, and what he helps organisations become capable of.

From making interfaces better to making design capability stronger.

The recurring pattern across Amit's career is not one domain or one visual style. It is the impulse to understand a system, see what is missing, make it better, and help people move with it.

Working hypothesis

Design leader who builds clarity across complexity, connects design with business and users, and helps organisations adapt to AI without losing the human edge.

“I turn complexity into clarity, and clarity into capability.”
Use this as a thinking anchor, not necessarily as final public copy.

02 / The story

Your strongest personal brand starts before the résumé. It starts with the pattern that has been present for a long time.

The early signal

As a student who loved maths, you saw a project demonstrating many mathematical axioms and theorems. Rather than simply admire it, you tried to make something that could demonstrate even more in a smaller, better form. It failed. The curiosity did not.

The recurring pattern

Observe what exists → question why it works that way → experiment → make it clearer or better → learn from what failed → keep pushing.

Curiosity

You naturally investigate how things fit together.

Improvement

You rarely accept the first workable version as the final one.

Capability

You increasingly care about making people and organisations better at design, not only making individual outputs.

06 / Positioning

The goal is not to claim everything. It is to make the intersection you uniquely occupy easier to recognise.

Potential positioning territory

Design leadership for complex, AI-enabled organisations.

With emphasis on systems, people, product experience and capability.

Distinctive intersection

Human-centred × systems-minded × business-aware × future-facing.

The value is in the combination.

Positioning questions

1
What problem do I repeatedly find myself wanting to solve?
Look beyond job descriptions. Look for the problem beneath projects.
2
What do I notice that others often miss?
This can become the foundation of a point of view.
3
What do people seek me out for?
Collect actual examples from colleagues, peers and mentees.
4
What can I credibly teach because I have lived it?
Credibility grows faster from experience than from trend commentary.

08 / Personal brand system

Think of the brand as a system of repeated signals, not a collection of polished assets.

Think

Your frameworks, hypotheses, questions, essays and talks.

Make

Your work, experiments, prototypes, systems and case studies.

Teach

Workshops, mentoring, academia, community and design education.

Brand principle

Depth before visibility.

Create fewer, stronger ideas and let repetition build recognition.

Content territory

Design leadership in the AI era • compositional / generative UI • design systems as context • organisational design capability • the human edge • business-design fluency • community and education.

10 / Next moves

The next step is not to polish a bio. It is to create the evidence and point of view that make the bio inevitable.

Build the intellectual spine

Define 3-5 ideas you want to become associated with. Write one strong argument for each. Test them through talks, workshops and conversations.

Build the proof library

Capture cases, outcomes, lessons, frameworks, artefacts and stories. Convert lived experience into reusable material.

Career

Position for broader product + business leadership.

Visibility

Publish with a consistent point of view, not a content treadmill.

Legacy

Keep building systems, communities and learning experiences that outlast individual projects.

The question is not “How do I build a personal brand?”
It is “What do I want to be trusted for, and what evidence will make that trust deserved?”
